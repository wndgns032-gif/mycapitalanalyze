Digital storefront gross revenues frequently conceal the financial realities facing independent creators. Omer, co-founder of two-person studio Northend Games, demonstrated this discrepancy by publishing an itemized balance sheet for Northend Tower Defense（诺森德塔防）. Over five years, the project generated over $560,000, yet Omer netted only $73,000, or about $14,600 per year.

## An Itemized Ledger of Platform Fees, Taxes, and Refunds

The breakdown posted to Reddit and YouTube outlines how deductions eroded gross receipts. Player refunds eliminated $134,000 immediately. Steam games normally see 10% to 15% refund rates, but Northend Tower Defense reached 22%, tied to high expectations in China. Storefront operator Valve then took its 30% platform cut of $127,000, leaving $297,000.

Cross-border taxes and publishing deals claimed the rest. The US Internal Revenue Service applies a 30% withholding tax on American sales for developers in Iraq. Moving to Thailand lowered this to 5% under a bilateral treaty, totaling $9,700, while Thai digital product taxes took $26,000. Regional publisher Gamersky took $115,000 for East Asian distribution, leaving $146,000 in net profit split equally between both co-founders.

## Community Pushback and the Realities of Market Expansion

The figures generated heated debate across online development communities. Critics argued Omer should not view customer refunds, partner revenue splits, or standard income taxes as unexpected losses, particularly since the project out-earned 90% of games on Steam. Forum moderators intervened to remove abusive comments targeting the developers.

However, the balance sheet illustrates the genuine logistical cost of global distribution. Founded in Iraq in 2022 amid post-war turbulence, Northend Games endured month-long electrical blackouts during Early Access before moving to Thailand. Partnering with Gamersky secured 64.9% of sales in China and helped eliminate illicit mobile copies, but required giving up significant revenue.

## Indie Developers Weigh Self-Publishing Against Reach

Northend Games has started working on its next title and plans to self-publish rather than hire outside agencies. Operating independently avoids external publisher revenue splits, yet forces a two-person team to shoulder community support, global PR, and anti-piracy enforcement alone. Industry observers are tracking whether bypassing third parties can genuinely improve take-home margins in an increasingly crowded PC marketplace.
