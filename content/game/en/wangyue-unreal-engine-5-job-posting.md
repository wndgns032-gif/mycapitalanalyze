## A Sudden Shift in Engine Strategy

A new job posting for Wangyue (望月), the flagship anime-style open-world game from Chinese studio Shiyue, has raised eyebrows. The listing, for a "UE Client Lead Programmer," describes the project as a "UE5 self-developed anime urban open-world ARPG." This marks a departure from previous official statements and media reports that identified the game as a Unity project. The timing is notable: the game is scheduled to begin PC online testing in the fourth quarter of this year. The posting offers a salary of 100–200K RMB per month at 16 months' pay, translating to over a million RMB annually, a package that suggests a genuine, high-level hire rather than a typo.

## Why It Matters

Wangyue has had a troubled development. Started in 2021, the game underwent a 15-month rebuild that concluded last October, expanding the team to over 600 people. At that time, Shiyue emphasized upgrades to the Unity HDRP rendering pipeline, with custom code modifications to improve visual quality on PC. The game held two offline playtests this summer in Guangzhou and Shanghai, and as recently as September, the company was still hiring for "U3D client development" roles. The sudden appearance of a UE5 position suggests a major technical pivot at a critical stage.

Shiyue's previous two anime titles, Yongye Jianglin: Fusu and Poxiao Xulie, underperformed expectations, making the company more cautious. Meanwhile, the anime game market has become increasingly competitive. Major titles like Arknights: Endfield (明日方舟：终末地) and NTE (异环) launched earlier this year, while Blue Star原: Lvyao (蓝色星原：旅谣) has announced a public beta for November 26, and NetEase's Infinite (无限大) is set for January 15, 2027. For a game that has already been in development for five years, any delay could be costly.

## What to Watch Next

The immediate question is whether the UE5 job posting signals a full engine switch or a parallel development effort. Switching engines mid-project is risky, especially with a PC test planned for the fourth quarter. The original Unity team would need to adapt, either by learning UE5 or transitioning to other roles. This could push back the release timeline, which already seemed uncertain after executive producer Hua Tao stepped down in September to become an honorary producer.

Shiyue's commitment to improving visual fidelity is clear, but the market won't wait. With competitors accelerating, Wangyue's ability to deliver a polished, distinctive experience will determine its success. The next few months, including the planned PC test, will reveal whether this latest pivot is a strategic upgrade or a sign of deeper troubles.
