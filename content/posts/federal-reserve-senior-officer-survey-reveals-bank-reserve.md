The Federal Reserve has published the findings of its most recent Senior Financial Officer Survey, shedding light on how major banks are steering reserve balances, shaping balance‑sheet expectations, and navigating money‑market activity as monetary policy evolves.

## Key Facts
- The questionnaire was fielded from September 18 to September 28, 2026, in partnership with the New York Fed.
- Respondents represent institutions that collectively hold roughly 75 % of total reserve balances in the U.S. banking system.
- Topics covered include reserve‑balance management tactics, projected size of balance sheets and deposit bases, participation in money‑market instruments, and operational approaches for weekends and holidays.
- The survey serves as a regular feedback channel for the Board to gauge the banking sector’s liquidity posture and expectations.
- A PDF version of the full survey results is available on the Fed’s website.

## Analysis
Banks appear to be calibrating their reserve‑holding behavior to the Fed’s ongoing balance‑sheet normalization and higher policy rates. The majority indicated a preference for maintaining a modest buffer above regulatory minimums, reflecting concerns about potential funding squeezes in the overnight market. Simultaneously, several institutions reported expanding their use of short‑term Treasury repos and commercial paper to offset lower excess reserves, a move that could tighten liquidity in money‑market funds if demand outpaces supply.

The data also hint at a gradual shift toward more active weekend and holiday liquidity planning. With the Fed’s recent experiments in extending payment‑system availability beyond traditional business days, banks are testing alternative funding sources to avoid overnight gaps. This behavior may smooth out seasonal volatility but could also introduce new risk vectors if counterparties are less vetted for off‑hour transactions.

From an investor perspective, the survey suggests that banks are likely to keep net interest margins under pressure for the near term, as they balance higher funding costs against a competitive loan‑pricing environment. Fixed‑income traders should monitor the evolving composition of banks’ reserve assets, as a tilt toward higher‑yielding money‑market instruments could influence short‑term Treasury yields and repo rates.

## Implications
- **Policy Outlook:** The Fed may interpret the modest increase in reserve buffers as a sign that the current rate path remains appropriate, reducing the urgency for further hikes.
- **Liquidity Management:** Market participants should expect continued reliance on repo markets, which may experience heightened sensitivity to supply‑side shocks.
- **Risk Monitoring:** Regulators and supervisors might focus more on banks’ weekend liquidity frameworks, ensuring that off‑hour funding arrangements meet prudential standards.

## FAQ
### What proportion of total system reserves do the surveyed banks hold?
They collectively account for about three‑quarters of all reserve balances held by U.S. banks.

### Why is the Fed interested in banks’ weekend liquidity practices?
Extended weekend operations can affect the stability of overnight funding markets, so the Fed wants to gauge how banks mitigate potential gaps.

### How could the survey results influence short‑term interest rates?
If banks shift more of their reserves into higher‑yielding money‑market assets, demand for short‑term Treasuries may rise, putting upward pressure on yields and repo rates.
