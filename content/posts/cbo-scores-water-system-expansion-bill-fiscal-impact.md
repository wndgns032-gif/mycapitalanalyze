The Congressional Budget Office (CBO) has released a cost estimate for S. 3725, the Lewis and Clark Regional Water System Expansion Feasibility Study Act, as reported by the Senate Committee on Energy and Natural Resources on September 17, 2026. While the bill itself is narrow in scope, it offers a window into how Washington approaches water infrastructure and the fiscal constraints shaping such projects.

## Key Facts

- The legislation would authorize a feasibility study for expanding the Lewis and Clark Regional Water System, a multi-state water delivery project in the Upper Midwest.
- The CBO's estimate, published on September 22, 2026, assesses the bill's impact on federal spending and revenues.
- The study would examine the potential for enlarging the system's capacity to serve additional communities and industrial users.
- The bill was reported by the Senate Energy and Natural Resources Committee, indicating bipartisan interest in water infrastructure development.
- No significant federal outlays are expected beyond the cost of the study itself, which would be subject to annual appropriations.

## Analysis

At first glance, a feasibility study may seem like a minor legislative item, but it carries broader economic significance. Water infrastructure is a foundational input for agriculture, energy production, and residential growth in the regions it serves. The Lewis and Clark system already supplies water to parts of South Dakota, Iowa, and Minnesota, and an expansion could support economic development in these areas.

From a macro perspective, the bill reflects a continuing trend: federal lawmakers are increasingly focused on targeted infrastructure investments that can unlock regional growth without massive upfront federal spending. The feasibility study is a low-cost way to signal support for a project that may eventually require significant federal and local funding. The CBO's role is to ensure that any such authorization does not add materially to the deficit in the near term.

Markets should note that water infrastructure projects often involve public-private partnerships and municipal bonds. If the study leads to a larger expansion, it could spur issuance of tax-exempt debt, impacting the municipal bond market. Additionally, construction and engineering firms with expertise in water systems could see new contract opportunities. However, the timeline is long: feasibility studies typically take years, and actual construction would require further congressional authorization and appropriations.

## Implications

For investors, the immediate impact is negligible, but the longer-term signal is that water infrastructure remains a priority for federal policymakers. This could benefit companies involved in water treatment, pipeline construction, and related technologies. For policymakers, the bill underscores the challenge of balancing fiscal responsibility with the need to maintain and expand critical infrastructure. The CBO's estimate will be used by lawmakers to assess whether the study's cost is justified, but the real fiscal question is whether the expansion itself will eventually be funded.

Moreover, the bill's progress through committee suggests that water projects can still garner bipartisan support, even in a polarized Congress. That is a positive sign for other infrastructure initiatives, though the path to enactment remains uncertain. The CBO's score is a necessary step, but it does not guarantee floor action.

## FAQ

### What does the CBO estimate say about the bill's cost?
The CBO estimates that the bill would have a minimal federal cost, primarily for the feasibility study, which would be subject to annual appropriations. No significant new spending or revenue changes are expected.

### How could this bill affect the municipal bond market?
If the study leads to an expansion, it could result in new municipal bond issuance to finance construction. That would increase supply in the tax-exempt market, potentially affecting yields for similar projects.

### Why is a feasibility study important for macroeconomics?
Feasibility studies are the first step in large infrastructure projects that can boost regional productivity, create jobs, and attract private investment. They signal where future federal dollars may flow, which markets watch closely.
