Joe Rogan, the Manosphere, and Trump’s 2024 Outlook After the Midterm Shock

Rogan blames the unpopular Iran conflict for Republican setbacks, highlighting Trump’s distancing from the manosphere and its ripple effects on markets and policy.

Source: Forbes Business

Intro

The recent U.S. midterm elections delivered a stark rebuke to many Republican candidates, and the fallout is reverberating far beyond the Capitol Hill corridors. Prominent media personality Joe Rogan linked the loss to the lingering unpopularity of the ongoing Iran war, while former President Donald Trump appears to be recalibrating his relationship with the so‑called “manosphere” – a loose network of influencers, podcasts and online forums that once amplified his brand.

Key Facts

  • Rogan’s assessment: In a post‑election interview, Rogan cited the “deeply unpopular” war in Iran as a primary factor eroding Republican support in swing districts.
  • Trump’s shift: The former president has begun to distance himself from the manosphere, a coalition that helped boost his 2016 and 2020 campaigns but has grown contentious after the midterms.
  • Midterm outcome: Republicans lost several key battleground seats, narrowing their majority in the House and raising doubts about the party’s ability to pass its agenda before the 2024 presidential race.
  • Market reaction: Equity indices slipped modestly on election night, while the VIX spiked, reflecting heightened uncertainty about fiscal policy and upcoming elections.
  • Policy backdrop: The Iran conflict, now entering its second year, has strained U.S. defense spending and contributed to higher energy prices, feeding into broader inflation concerns.

    • Analysis

    The convergence of a protracted foreign engagement and a fracturing political base creates a multi‑layered risk environment for investors. First, the war’s unpopularity is translating into a political cost for any candidate perceived as endorsing continued involvement, which could depress defense‑related stocks if congressional support wanes. Second, Trump’s retreat from the manosphere signals a strategic pivot: he may be seeking to broaden his appeal to suburban and moderate voters ahead of 2024, potentially reshaping the Republican platform toward more centrist economic policies.

    From a macro‑economic perspective, the midterm setbacks could slow the passage of tax cuts or spending bills that the GOP has championed, thereby tempering fiscal stimulus. This, in turn, may ease upward pressure on inflation, which has been partly fueled by higher oil prices linked to the Iran conflict. Bond markets are already pricing in a modest reduction in the risk premium for U.S. Treasuries, as investors anticipate a less aggressive fiscal stance.

    However, the lingering uncertainty around the war’s duration keeps the risk premium elevated. Should the conflict intensify, commodity markets—particularly oil and gas—could see renewed volatility, feeding back into consumer price dynamics and prompting the Federal Reserve to reconsider its tightening cycle.

    Implications

    • For investors: Diversify exposure away from sectors heavily dependent on defense contracts and energy inputs. Consider quality dividend stocks and short‑duration bonds to hedge against policy‑driven volatility.
    • For policymakers: A more centrist Republican agenda may open space for bipartisan dialogue on fiscal responsibility, potentially easing the path for targeted infrastructure spending without reigniting inflation.
    • For the 2024 race: Trump’s recalibration could reshape the primary battlefield, forcing other GOP contenders to distance themselves from the more extreme elements of the manosphere and focus on economic credibility.

      • FAQ

      What does Rogan’s comment imply for future U.S. foreign policy?

      Rogan’s view underscores growing public fatigue with prolonged conflicts, suggesting that lawmakers may face mounting pressure to seek diplomatic exits or limit further military commitments.

      How might Trump’s distancing from the manosphere affect his voter base?

      By moving away from the more polarizing online sub‑cultures, Trump could attract moderate Republicans and independents, but he risks alienating a segment of his core supporters who value the anti‑establishment rhetoric.

      Should investors adjust their portfolios now?

      A prudent approach would involve reducing concentration in defense and energy‑sensitive equities, while increasing holdings in sectors less tied to geopolitical risk, such as consumer staples and technology with strong cash flows.
