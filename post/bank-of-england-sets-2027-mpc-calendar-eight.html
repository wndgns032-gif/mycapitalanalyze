Bank of England Sets 2027 MPC Calendar: Eight Meetings, Four Reports, and a 2018 Transcript Release

The Bank of England has confirmed its 2027 Monetary Policy Committee meeting dates, pairing four sessions with Monetary Policy Reports and scheduling the next eight-year-delayed transcript release for January 2027.

Source: Bank of England

The Bank of England has published its 2027 Monetary Policy Committee calendar, giving markets a full year of advance notice on when rate decisions, minutes, and quarterly forecasts will land. The schedule preserves the MPC's eight-meeting rhythm and its practice of publishing a Monetary Policy Report alongside four of those decisions.

Key Facts

  • The 2027 MPC announcement dates are 4 February, 18 March, 29 April, 17 June, 29 July, 16 September, 4 November, and 16 December.
  • Minutes are published simultaneously with each announcement, so there is no separate wait for the vote breakdown.
  • Monetary Policy Reports accompany four meetings: 4 February, 29 April, 29 July, and 4 November.
  • Under the transparency framework introduced after the 2014 Warsh Review, the Bank releases meeting transcripts and key staff inputs after an eight-year delay.
  • The next such release is 7 January 2027, covering all MPC meetings held between January and December 2018.
  • The transcript release policy mirrors the approach used by The UK National Archives.

    • Analysis

    A published calendar is not a policy signal, but it is a piece of market infrastructure. Fixed-income desks, FX strategists, and corporate treasurers use these dates to schedule hedging decisions, position around event risk, and avoid launching issuance into an MPC afternoon. The 2027 list is unremarkable in cadence — eight decisions, four forecast rounds — which itself matters: continuity reduces the chance of surprise scheduling changes that can distort short-dated rate expectations.

    The more interesting item is the 7 January 2027 transcript release. It will expose the internal debate from 2018, a year when the Bank was navigating the tail end of post-referendum uncertainty and gradually normalising from emergency settings. Transcripts are backward-looking and cannot move today's curve directly, but they shape how economists model the MPC's reaction function. If the 2018 record shows a committee more divided than the published minutes suggested, or more focused on a variable that markets underweight, it can influence how traders interpret the current voting patterns.

    For investors, the four Monetary Policy Report dates — 4 February, 29 April, 29 July, and 4 November — are the highest-volatility sessions. Those meetings combine a rate decision with fresh growth and inflation projections and a press conference, giving the Governor room to reshape the narrative. The four non-report meetings are more likely to be holding actions unless data forces a move.

    For policy watchers, the eight-year transcript lag is a deliberate compromise between accountability and candour. Officials speak more freely when they know the record stays sealed for nearly a decade. That improves the quality of internal debate but delays public understanding of how decisions were really made. The 2027 release will be a test of whether that trade-off still looks right in an era of faster information cycles.

    If you report under UK EMIR, the practical question is whether the 7 January 2027 transcript release will reveal a 2018 committee more divided than the minutes suggested — a possibility that could shift how you model the MPC's reaction function.

    FAQ

    How many times will the MPC meet in 2027?

    Eight times, on the dates listed above, with minutes published at the same time as each decision.

    When is the next Monetary Policy Report due?

    The first of four 2027 reports is scheduled for 4 February, alongside that day's rate announcement.

    What is released on 7 January 2027?

    Transcripts and key staff inputs from all MPC meetings held in 2018, issued under the Bank's eight-year disclosure rule.
